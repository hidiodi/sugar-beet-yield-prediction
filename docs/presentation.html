Dynamic Regime Switching
for Pre-Season Crop Yield Forecasting
Integrating Biophysical Limits with Machine Learning
Tim Schweitzer  ·  Germany
Sugarbeet · 2005–2024 · N = 3,794 district-years
Welcome. Today I am presenting our work on dynamic regime switching for crop yield forecasting. The core challenge we address is how to build an operational forecast that issues reliable predictions before the season even begins, navigating the extreme uncertainty of long-range weather forecasting.
Motivation

The Forecasting Problem

  • Operational Constraint: Forecasts must be issued before sowing, using only antecedent soil states and highly uncertain seasonal forecasts.
  • Interpolation vs Extrapolation: Statistical models learn long-term tech trends perfectly, but fail completely during climate shocks (e.g., 2018 drought).
  • The ML Dilemma: Machine learning algorithms overfit to historical variance and struggle to extrapolate novel shocks without hard physiological boundaries.
  • The Data Challenge: The feature space is highly dimensional yet historically sparse, making unconstrained machine learning models extremely susceptible to overfitting.
Our constraint is strict: predict the harvest on March 1st. As the chart shows, European yields have a strong technological upward trend, punctuated by severe crashes like 2003 and 2018. Statistical trend lines miss these crashes entirely. Pure ML overfits the noise. We needed an architecture that defaults to the trend, but switches to physics when limits are breached.
Theory

Yield Formation as a Regime-Switching Process

  • Normal Regime Driven by secular technological trends and minor weather variance. Statistical interpolation dominates.
  • Extreme Stress Driven by hard physiological bottlenecks (e.g., heat damage, xylem cavitation). Historical trends become uninformative.
Empirical Confirmation:
A standalone XGBoost model forced to learn both regimes simultaneously achieved a skill score of −17.4% vs the baseline. Unconstrained ML in a high-variance agricultural setting actively degrades performance.
Our core hypothesis is that crop yield operates in distinct regimes. In normal years, the trend is king. In stress years, physics take over. We empirically confirmed this: an un-gated XGBoost model performed 17.4% worse than a simple trend line. A model that cannot distinguish regimes is worse than doing nothing.
Architecture

Three Components, One Asymmetric Gate

Asymmetric Gate Logic:

If ML deviates < −0.04
  → 100% Trust ML

If ML deviates > +0.04
  → 50% ML / 50% Trend

Else
  → 100% Trust Trend
Crashes are governed by hard physiological bounds (extrapolation). Bumpers are harder to guarantee pre-season (interpolation limits). We enforce a conservative 50% constraint on the upside.
To constrain the ML, we designed an asymmetric gate. The Trend is the default. We only trigger the Hybrid when it deviates strongly. We fully trust it on the downside because droughts hit hard physiological limits. We penalize it 50% on the upside to prevent ML extrapolation hallucination. But how do we build the features that trigger this gate?
Feature Engineering

Extracting Signal: The Custom Heat Model ($S_{heat}$)

  • The Problem: European long-range seasonal weather forecasts (SEAS5) issued in March have near-zero correlation with actual summer weather.
  • The Solution: We built a dedicated XGBoost sub-model to predict "Days > 25°C".
  • We engineered non-linear interactions between SEAS5 ensembles and local soil characteristics (e.g., heat_sand_interaction).
Result: By transforming raw, noisy meteorological data into a targeted agronomic metric, our sub-model achieves $r = 0.63$ pre-season, capturing the majority of predictable signal.
One of the major engineering hurdles is that long-range seasonal forecasts issued in March are notoriously unreliable. If we fed raw SEAS5 data into our yield model, it would learn nothing. Instead, we built a dedicated XGBoost sub-model that learns the interaction between SEAS5 signals and static soil properties, achieving a correlation of 0.63. We extract the signal from the noise before it ever hits the main yield model.
Feature Engineering

Grounding ML in Physics: The WOFOST Ensemble

  • Initialization: We initialize the WOFOST crop simulator on March 1st using actual district-level soil moisture and antecedent winter conditions.
  • The Unknown Future: Because we do not know the summer weather, we run 30 parallel simulations using historical weather traces (1995-2024).
The Output: This ensemble generates a probability distribution of potential yields. We extract the 10th percentile (p10) as a Downside Risk feature, giving the XGBoost model a hard, physiologically-calculated boundary for droughts.
To integrate biophysical limits, we don't just feed raw weather to an ML model. On March 1st, we initialize the WOFOST simulator with the actual antecedent winter conditions. We then run 30 parallel simulations representing historical weather trajectories. The resulting ensemble spread allows us to extract the 10th percentile downside risk, grounding the XGBoost model in a mathematically rigorous physiological boundary.
Validation

The Leak-Free Standard

  • Walk-forward time-series CV, 2005–2024. Zero future information permitted.
  • Economic prices strictly lagged t−1 (autumn purchasing decisions available March 1st).
Economic leakage (contemporaneous prices) falsely inflates skill by +3–5 percentage points. Our reported skill represents genuinely achievable operational performance.
We evaluate strictly via Walk-Forward Validation. When we predict 2018, the model has only seen years up to 2017. Many retrospective studies use contemporaneous commodity prices as proxies for management intensity. We explicitly quantified that this "look-ahead bias" inflates skill by 3-5 points. Our results are grounded in what is actually operational on March 1st.

Empirical Boundary Conditions

The Error Butterfly

  • The aggregate MAE improvement is driven entirely by the extreme crash regime (<500 dt/ha).
  • In normal and bumper yield years, the statistical trend is highly efficient and should not be overridden.
Strategic Shift: Because the system functions as a crash-regime specialist, evaluating it purely on aggregate regression RMSE obscures its operational value.
Look at the Error Butterfly. The baseline trend is highly accurate in the middle, but explodes at the extremes. Our model sacrifices accuracy in the middle to aggressively tame the downside tail. Because of this explicit trade-off, we must evaluate this model as an anomaly detector — not a general regression model.
Anomaly Detection

Evaluating the Crash Specialist

  • Crash Event: District yield drops ≥10% below trend forecast.
  • The trend model achieves 0% Recall by definition.
Our physically-gated system successfully catches 53.8% of all district crashes pre-season, providing actionable early warnings for catastrophic agricultural risk.
Precision = 19.7%: approximately 4 in 5 crash predictions are false alarms. Acceptable for insurance and supply-chain risk management; requires calibration for farm-level decisions.
When we view this as a classification problem, the value is clear. The trend catches 0% of crashes by construction. Our model catches over 53% of catastrophic district failures pre-season. Precision is ~20% — we issue false alarms. But in agricultural risk management, a false positive means unnecessary caution; a false negative means an uninsured crop failure.

Conclusion

Summary & Future Work

Key Contributions: We engineered highly predictive pre-season features ($S_{heat}$ $r=0.63$) and introduced a leak-free, physically-gated architecture that catches 53.8% of district crashes. It successfully detects structural crop vulnerability from antecedent winter signals.
Empirical Boundary Conditions: The system is a crash-regime specialist; it generates a high false-alarm rate in normal years. Tree-based integration is highly vulnerable to socio-economic covariate shifts.
Future Work:
  • Substitute flat feature spaces for hard biophysical override gates.
  • Separate biological feature streams from economic management variables.
  • Inject Monte Carlo synthetic weather into WOFOST to train the ML model on unprecedented extremes.
To conclude, we built a system that extracts massive pre-season signal from noisy data. It proves that to predict black swan events, we must let machine learning handle the complex non-linear center, but rigorously subordinate it to hard biophysical limits at the extremes.
