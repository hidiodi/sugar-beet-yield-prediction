\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Interpretation of Results}
The Super Ensemble demonstrates superior performance (16--18\% skill gain) by effectively switching between component models. The ``Soft Voting'' mechanism allows it to leverage the stability of the Trend model during normal years while shifting weight to Physics-Informed components (like the Robust Linear Integrator) during extreme years (2003, 2018).

\subsection{Signal-to-Noise Ratio}
The Meta-Learner's ability to successfully diagnose and predict extreme outcomes is primarily attributable to two implemented factors: the high informational value of the complex engineered features (e.g., Z-scores, Scorch Index, Anoxia) and the critical role of the \textbf{Data Quality Control} filter. By removing training rows with an Oracle Error $> 200 \text{ dt/ha}$, the system was prevented from overfitting to likely data entry errors, ensuring the classifier focused on distinguishing between genuine, climate-driven failures.

\subsection{Comparison with Baselines and Literature}
The model consistently outperforms the Statistical Trend baseline. It is important to note that this baseline is not a simple linear regression but a sophisticated GAM + ARIMA pipeline, representing a high-quality, time-series-expert benchmark. The widening performance gap in the ``Recent Volatility'' period (2010--2024) indicates that even robust statistical extrapolation is becoming less reliable as climate patterns shift, necessitating the physics-informed hybrid approach.

Our results align with and extend recent findings in the field. For example, \citet{paudel2021machine} reported that while machine learning baselines are competitive with operational systems for various crops in Europe, they often struggle with extreme anomalies. Our approach directly addresses this by explicitly engineering mechanism-based stress signals (e.g., Anoxia, Scorch) and employing a Meta-Learner to detect these ``Physics Regimes''.

\subsection{Uncertainty Quantification}
While the primary output of the Super Ensemble is a point forecast, the underlying Physics-Informed Ensemble generates quantile predictions (0.20 and 0.80). These quantiles provide a valuable probabilistic envelope, defining the ``Pessimistic Floor'' and ``Optimistic Ceiling'' for the yield. For agricultural stakeholders, this uncertainty range is as critical as the mean forecast, allowing for risk-based decision making (e.g., hedging against the 0.20 quantile outcome).

\subsection{Limitations}
Despite the promising results, this study faces several limitations. First, the reliance on interpolated weather grids \cite{rauthe2013central} introduces uncertainty, particularly for precipitation-driven features like Anoxia. Anoxia is often driven by intense, short-duration thunderstorms. Interpolated grids tend to smooth these localized extremes over space and time, potentially under-representing the true intensity of waterlogging events in specific fields.
Second, aggregating data to the district level may mask significant within-district variability in soil types and management practices. Finally, while the Meta-Learner effectively handles known stress patterns, its ability to generalize to completely unprecedented compound extremes (e.g., a new pest outbreak combined with heat) remains an open challenge.
