\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Regime Selection Efficiency and Benchmark Failure}
The Physically-Gated Anomaly Detector achieves a \textbf{1.93\%} skill gain (3.21\% in recent years) by employing an Asymmetric Threshold Gating strategy. In contrast, the pure Machine Learning components (Model A, Model C) failed to consistently improve upon the statistical trend (Skill: $<0\%$). This result highlights the limitations of "unleashed" machine learning in high-variance, low-signal-to-noise agricultural environments. The Gate's superior performance confirms that dynamic regime switching—explicitly separating "Normal" from "Extreme" logic—is essential for capturing non-stationary climate impacts that static ensembles miss.

\subsection{Uncertainty and Drivers}
The system's diagnostic capability is driven by the $M_{hybrid}$ Hybrid Detector, which integrates physical priors from WOFOST. Forensic analysis confirms that the underlying regime dynamics are driven by complex mechanism-informed features like `sowing_doy` and `heat_signal`. While the operational Gate relies on a simplified threshold logic ($|Dev| > 4.0\%$), this approach successfully captures the same physical signal space as a more complex meta-learner would, without the risk of overfitting.

\subsection{Limitations and Anomaly Forensics}
Limitations include reliance on interpolated weather grids, which smooth localized extremes. However, the system's conservative nature prevents over-reaction. For instance, in the 2003 heatwave, the Physically-Gated Anomaly Detector defaulted to the trend baseline (593.1 dt/ha). While this missed the full extent of the crash (Actual: 516.2), it avoided the catastrophic failure modes of pure ML models (which predicted NaNs or physical impossibilities). This highlights a trade-off: the Gate prioritizes robustness against false alarms (over-prediction of crashes) at the cost of occasionally missing the full extent of legitimate extremes when signals are ambiguous.

Conversely, the 2018 drought error reduction (Trend 797 vs Gate 743) demonstrates the value of the "Crash Mode". The strong physical signals (Late Sowing + Dry Soil) triggered the 100\% trust mechanism, successfully pulling the forecast down towards reality (625.5).

\subsection{Methodological Considerations}

\subsubsection{The Leak-Free Reality}
By strictly enforcing the Leak-Free Standard (lagging prices $T-1$), we accept lower nominal skill scores (1.93\% vs ~8\%) in exchange for operational integrity. This ensures that the system is not "cheating" with future information, providing a true test of pre-season predictability.

\subsubsection{Safety Mode Rationale}
The Asymmetric Gate includes a "Safety Mode" (Trend Default) that reverts to the statistical trend during ambiguous signals. This is a necessary "Min-Max Regret" strategy for pre-season forecasting. Early-season signals (March 1st) for extreme crashes have high uncertainty; false alarms can be economically more damaging than missing a crash. The 50\% trust in "Bumper" signals reflects this caution—upside potential is harder to guarantee than downside risk.
