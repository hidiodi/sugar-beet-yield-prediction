\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Interpretation and Efficiency}
The Super Ensemble achieves a 16.16\% skill gain by effectively switching strategies. Analysis reveals that 71.6\% of residual error is systemic (Component Gap), while only 28.4\% is due to incorrect model selection (Fig.~\ref{fig:switching}). This validates the Meta-Learner's efficiency and directs future research toward improving sub-model physics.

Unlike monitoring-based systems like MCYFS that rely on in-season data \citep{vandervelde2019mars}, our use of SEAS5 on March 1st provides a critical 90-day window for economic hedging. While quantile predictions ($M_2$) offer a probabilistic envelope, the primary value lies in the early detection of yield crashes.

\subsection{Limitations}
Limitations include reliance on interpolated weather grids \cite{rauthe2013central}, which smooth localized extremes, and district-level aggregation masking field variability.

\subsection{Methodological Considerations}

\subsubsection{Cross-Validation Rigor}
The expanding window protocol ensures strict temporal separation between training and test sets, mimicking operational deployment where only historical data informs future forecasts. Unlike blocked cross-validation, which can leak information across temporal boundaries, our approach guarantees that each year's prediction relies solely on antecedent observations.

\subsubsection{Data Integrity and Outlier Handling}
An early iteration of the system employed a retrospective outlier filter based on multi-model consensus, flagging district-years where all components exhibited errors exceeding 200~dt/ha. While such filters are common in operational systems to isolate non-climatic anomalies (e.g., misreported yields), we opted to exclude this step to avoid introducing circularity into the validation framework. The negligible performance impact of this exclusion (Section~3.4) validates our decision and demonstrates that the system's skill derives from capturing genuine climatic signals rather than selectively curating validation data.

\subsubsection{Hyperparameter Selection}
Meta-Learner hyperparameters were chosen conservatively to prioritize generalization over training-set fit. The shallow tree depth (\texttt{max\_depth=4}) and low learning rate (\texttt{learning\_rate=0.03}) prevent overfitting to idiosyncratic district-year patterns, while the ensemble variance and historical bias features provide regime-specific context that cannot be trivially memorized.
