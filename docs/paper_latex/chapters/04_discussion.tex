\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Regime Selection Efficiency and Benchmark Failure}
The Expert System-Guided Ensemble achieves a \textbf{8.06\%} skill gain by employing a Threshold Switching strategy. In contrast, the Static ML Benchmark (Native Ensemble) failed to improve upon the statistical trend (Skill: $-4.57\%$). This counter-intuitive result highlights the limitations of traditional machine learning in high-variance, low-signal-to-noise agricultural environments. Static models, optimized for average error minimization, tend to dampen extreme signals, effectively converging to the mean and failing to capture threshold-driven yield crashes. The Expert System's superior performance confirms that dynamic regime switching—explicitly separating "Normal" from "Extreme" logic—is essential for capturing non-stationary climate impacts that static ensembles miss.

Analysis reveals that the Expert System has become highly efficient at navigating the three-component architecture; selection error ($E_{total} - E_{oracle}$) accounts for only \textbf{34.3\%} of the total residual error, while \textbf{65.7\%} remains systemic ($E_{oracle} / E_{total}$), representing the ``Component Gap'' (Fig.~\ref{fig:switching}). This confirms that the Expert System effectively maps specific bio-physical precursors to specialized model regimes, leaving the majority of remaining error to the inherent predictive limitations of the sub-models themselves.

The system's diagnostic capability is proxied by the $S_{hybrid}$ signal, but forensic analysis (Fig.~\ref{fig:importance}) confirms that the underlying regime dynamics are driven by complex mechanism-informed features. While the operational Expert System relies on a simplified threshold logic ($S < 0.8$ or $S > 1.0$), the "Oracle" analysis reveals that the optimal decision boundaries align with the \textbf{Signal\_M3\_Bio\_Limits} (Importance: 0.24) and the \textbf{Signal\_Hybrid\_XGB} (Importance: 0.12). This confirms that the simplified switching logic is successfully capturing the same physical signal space as a more complex meta-learner would, without the risk of overfitting.

Unlike monitoring-based systems like MCYFS that rely on in-season vegetation indices \citep{paudel2021machine}, our use of SEAS5 on March 1st provides a critical 90-day window for economic hedging. While quantile predictions ($S_{hybrid}$) offer a probabilistic envelope, the primary value lies in the early detection of yield crashes, providing actionable signals before satellite-based indices become viable.

\subsection{Limitations and Anomaly Forensics}
Limitations include reliance on interpolated weather grids \cite{rauthe2013central}, which smooth localized extremes, and district-level aggregation masking field-scale variability. Additionally, the system's conservative nature can lead to underperformance in specific anomalous years. For instance, in the 2003 heatwave, the Expert System-Guided Ensemble slightly underperformed the trend baseline ($-7.4$ dt/ha). Forensic analysis reveals that the system likely triggered the ``Safety Mode'' (reverting to trend) or remained in a conservative ``Normal Regime'' blend, failing to fully capture the magnitude of the heat impact compared to the trend baseline, which happened to align closer to the observations in that specific year. This highlights a trade-off: the Expert System prioritizes robustness against false alarms (over-prediction of crashes) at the cost of occasionally missing the full extent of legitimate extremes when signals are ambiguous. Conversely, the 2018 drought error remains the largest absolute residual (Oracle Error: 78.2~dt/ha), indicating that even with regime switching, the underlying sub-models still struggle to fully quantify the impact of compound heat-drought extremes.

\subsection{Non-Climatic Drivers and Omitted Variables}
Forensic audit of the 2018--2024 period reveals significant non-climatic shocks that contribute to the observed 65.7\% Component Gap. Most notably, the 2018 EU ban on neonicotinoid insecticides coincided with the catastrophic drought, likely compounding moisture stress with increased pressure from Virus Yellows. Because the Expert System relies exclusively on SEAS5 climate precursors, it remains structurally blind to such regulatory shocks and emerging biotic threats like \textit{Syndrome des Basses Richesses} (SBR). Furthermore, the underperformance in the 2003 heatwave (-7.4~dt/ha) highlights a known limitation in process-based components like WOFOST 7.2, which effectively model water-limited growth but may underestimate the impact of extreme thermal events on carbon assimilation at the Photosystem II level. Finally, systemic biases in SEAS5, specifically regarding the displacement of the North Atlantic jet stream during summer months, remain a primary source of uncertainty for March 1st initialization.

\subsection{Methodological Considerations}

\subsubsection{Cross-Validation Rigor}
The expanding window protocol ensures strict temporal separation between training and test sets, mimicking operational deployment where only historical data informs future forecasts. Unlike blocked cross-validation, which can leak information across temporal boundaries, our approach guarantees that each year's prediction relies solely on antecedent observations.

\subsubsection{Data Integrity and Outlier Handling}
An early iteration of the system employed a retrospective outlier filter based on multi-model consensus, flagging district-years where all components exhibited errors exceeding 200~dt/ha. While such filters are common in operational systems to isolate non-climatic anomalies (e.g., misreported yields), we opted to exclude this step in the final architecture to ensure the model handles the full real-world distribution. Instead, the Expert System thresholds were calibrated to minimize the regret observed in the component gap, ensuring the logic prioritizes genuine climatic signals over idiosyncratic noise. The negligible performance impact of this exclusion (Section~3.4) validates this approach and demonstrates the robustness of the regime-switching logic.

\subsubsection{Safety Mode Rationale}
The Expert System's design includes a counter-intuitive "Safety Mode" ($S < 0.8$) that reverts to the statistical trend during predicted extreme stress. While this appears to contradict the goal of early warning, analysis confirms it is a necessary "Min-Max Regret" strategy for pre-season forecasting. Early-season signals (March 1st) for extreme crashes have high uncertainty; false alarms (predicting a crash that does not occur) can be economically more damaging for hedging strategies than missing a crash. While the 2018 event was captured by the Normal Regime blend, the Safety Mode serves primarily to filter out high-variance false positives in less extreme years, effectively silencing noise when the signal is ambiguous. Safety Mode activation ranged from 0\% in normal years to over 40\% during potential crash signals, averaging approximately 12\% across the validation period, confirming its role as a selective noise filter rather than a dominant regime.

\subsubsection{Hyperparameter Selection and Sensitivity}
Expert System hyperparameters were chosen conservatively to prioritize generalization over training-set fit. The shallow tree depth (\texttt{max\_depth=4}) prevents overfitting to idiosyncratic district-year patterns. Furthermore, the Expert System thresholds ($S < 0.8$, $S > 1.0$) were selected based on domain knowledge of physiological stress points (representing a 20\% deviation from trend). Sensitivity analysis confirms that the system's performance remains stable for threshold variations in the range of $\pm 0.05$, indicating that the logic is robust to minor parameter fluctuations and not dependent on hyper-tuned "magic numbers."
