\section{Conclusions}

The Super Ensemble Hybrid Model developed in this study represents a robust solution for early-season crop yield forecasting. By utilizing a Meta-Learner with Soft Voting, the system successfully integrates process-based simulation with machine learning to address the "Interpolation vs. Extrapolation" problem inherent in standard models. The integration of lead-time stress markers and a Regret-Weighted training scheme allows the model to pivot toward physiological mechanisms exactly when the \textbf{Statistical Trend} fails.

The significant reduction in error during the 2018 drought validates the hypothesis that yield formation follows a regime-switching process. Furthermore, the forensic error decomposition indicates that the Meta-Learner has nearly optimized the selection process among the current sub-models. This suggests that the primary bottleneck for further skill gains is no longer the ensemble architecture, but rather the underlying bio-physical characterization within the sub-models.

Looking ahead, future research will shift focus from ensemble optimization to improving sub-model physics, specifically through site-specific parameterization of WOFOST 7.2 to close the identified "Oracle Gap." Additionally, we aim to extend this bio-physical risk scanning framework to other tuber crops and cereals to validate the generalizability of the regime-switching hypothesis. To further enhance the utility for risk managers, we plan to transition from traditional quantile regression toward conformal prediction methods, which provide rigorous, statistically sound coverage guarantees. Finally, we will focus on optimizing the simulation pipeline for cloud-based infrastructure to enable real-time inference for large-scale regional deployments.
