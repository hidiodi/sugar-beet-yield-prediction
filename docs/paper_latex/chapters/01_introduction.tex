\section{Introduction}

As climate change increases the frequency of extreme weather events, traditional statistical models and pure process-based models both face limitations. Statistical models, which rely largely on interpolation, often fail when conditions deviate from historical norms. Conversely, process-based models often struggle with district-level calibration and the uncertainty of initial soil conditions. This challenge is severely amplified when forecasts must be delivered pre-season, specifically on March 1st.

The core challenge is the ``Interpolation vs. Extrapolation'' dilemma. The necessity of a regime-switching approach was starkly illustrated during the 2018 European drought, where the unprecedented magnitude of yield anomalies across Northern Europe exposed the limitations of standard forecasting models in capturing compound extremes \citep{beillouin2020impact}.

Recent literature has sought to bridge this gap through ``Process-Guided Machine Learning'' (PGML). \citet{shahhosseini2021coupling} demonstrated that using crop simulation outputs—such as those from the \textbf{WOFOST} model \citep{boogaard1998wofost}—as features in machine learning could reduce error by 8--20\%. While such hybrid models can rival operational systems like MCYFS \citep{vandervelde2019mars}, they often remain conservative in extreme years. Our work extends this lineage by moving beyond static feature integration to a \textbf{dynamic ensemble} approach. Unlike static hybrids, our system explicitly acknowledges non-stationarity, switching its structural reliance from a \textbf{Statistical Trend} to physiological limits when precursor signals are detected.

We hypothesize that yield formation follows a ``Regime Switching'' process oscillating between two states. In a \textbf{Normal Regime}, yields are driven by gradual technological trends and minor weather fluctuations, allowing a \textbf{Statistical Trend} extrapolation to perform well. However, in an \textbf{Extreme Stress Regime}, yields are limited by distinct physiological bottlenecks—such as stomatal closure or root anoxia—that decouple outcomes from historical averages. Our novel contribution is a Meta-Learner that explicitly detects the active regime using seasonal forecast data from \textbf{ECMWF SEAS5} \citep{ecmwf_seas5} and reanalysis data from \textbf{AgERA5} \citep{agera5}, switching the forecasting strategy accordingly.
