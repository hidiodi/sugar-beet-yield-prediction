\section{Materials and Methods}

\subsection{Study Area and Data Integration}
The study area encompasses sugarbeet-cultivating districts in Germany ($d \in D$) for the harvest years $t \in T$ (1981--2024). Yield data ($Y_{d,t}$), measured in decitonnes per hectare (dt/ha), was harmonized from regional archives and modern Federal Statistical Office (Destatis) records. To isolate climatic signals from administrative noise, spatial masking was applied via the DLR CropMap \citep{dlr_cropmap}, aggregating meteorological variables strictly from relevant agricultural pixels.

\subsection{Non-Climatic Outlier Detection}
To ensure the system learns from climatic signals rather than reporting artifacts, we implemented a non-climatic noise filter. Observations were excluded from the training set if the minimum error achievable by any individual sub-model exceeded 200 dt/ha despite benign climatic forcing. These instances identify non-climatic yield shocks—such as administrative reporting errors or localized biotic failures—that fall outside the scope of atmospheric yield modeling. As shown in the ablation study (Section 3.4), the inclusion of this filter resulted in a negligible change ($<0.5\%$) in global Skill Score, confirming that the system's predictive power is derived from its architecture rather than data selection.

\subsection{The Statistical Trend Baseline}
The Statistical Trend baseline forecast, denoted as $\hat{y}_{\text{base}}(t)$, represents the expected yield under average climatic conditions. It is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{y}_{\text{base}}(t) = \alpha + f_s(t) + \hat{\epsilon}_{t}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha$ is the intercept, $f_s(t)$ is a cubic smoothing spline of the year $t$, and $\hat{\epsilon}_{t}$ is the predicted residual derived from an ARIMA(1,0,0) process.

\subsection{Feature Engineering and Variable Definitions}
We derive normalized Z-scores for primary climatic drivers to prevent look-ahead bias. For a feature $x$ at year $t$, the score $Z_t = (x_t - \mu_{1:t-1}) / \sigma_{1:t-1}$ is calculated. We specifically define $Z_{\text{heat}}$ (derived from days where $T_{\text{max}} > 25^{\circ}\text{C}$), $Z_{\text{bal}}$ (summer water balance), and $Z_{\text{tank}}$ (simulated winter soil water recharge).

These scores inform three cumulative physiological stress markers: the \textbf{Critical Dryness Index (CASDI)}, which counts days of simulated root-zone water exhaustion; the \textbf{Night-time Metabolic Stress Detector (NMSD)}, tracking biomass depletion via high minimum temperatures; and \textbf{Oxygen Stress (OSAW)}, counting days of simulated soil saturation inhibiting root respiration. Crucially, the March 1st lead-time relies on land-surface memory: $Z_{\text{tank}}$ serves as the boundary condition for CASDI, determining the time-to-exhaustion of the soil water reservoir months before planting.

\subsection{Predictive Sub-Models}
The ensemble integrates four specialized sub-models $M_k$ ($k \in \{1, \dots, 4\}$). The first ($M_1$) is the \textbf{Multivariate Heat Signal}, predicting $Z_{\text{heat}}$ via Spring precursors. The second ($M_2$) is the \textbf{Physics-Informed Ensemble}, a gradient-boosting hybrid providing quantile-based yield floors and ceilings. The third ($M_3$) is the \textbf{Hybrid XGBoost}, which utilizes WOFOST simulation outputs to predict yield; if $M_3$ generates a yield-to-trend ratio, it is rescaled by $\hat{y}_{\text{base}}$ to absolute units before ensemble integration. The fourth ($M_4$) is the \textbf{Robust Linear Integrator}, utilizing a HuberRegressor for stable residual prediction.

\subsection{Super Ensemble Architecture}
The system utilizes an XGBoost Meta-Learner as a dynamic gating network. The Meta-Learner is trained on a feature vector $\mathbf{x}_{d,t}$ encoding bio-physical context, historical bias, and ensemble variance.

\subsubsection{Regret-Weighted Training}
To prioritize the mitigation of extreme errors, training samples are weighted by the $\text{Regret}$ ($w_{d,t}$). Let $\text{E}_{d,t}$ be the absolute error of a prediction. The weight is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    w_{d,t} = \text{E}_{\text{median}}(d,t) - \text{E}_{\text{oracle}}(d,t)
\end{equation}
where $\text{E}_{\text{median}}$ is the median absolute error of the four sub-models and $\text{E}_{\text{oracle}}$ is the best-case error achieved by the optimal model:
\begin{equation}
    \text{E}_{\text{oracle}}(d,t) = \min_{k \in \{1,\dots,4\}} |Y_{d,t} - \hat{y}_k(d,t)|
\end{equation}
This ensures the Meta-Learner prioritizes learning from observations where a clear optimal choice existed to mitigate a failure of the Statistical Trend.

\subsubsection{Inference via Soft Voting}
The final yield forecast $\hat{Y}_{d,t}$ is the probability-weighted sum:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{Y}_{d,t} = \sum_{k=1}^{K} P(M_k | \mathbf{x}_{d,t}) \cdot \hat{y}_k(d,t)
\end{equation}
