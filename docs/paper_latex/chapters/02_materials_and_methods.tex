\section{Materials and Methods}

\subsection{Study Area and Datasets}
The study covers sugarbeet cultivation districts in Germany, integrating a multi-source dataset spanning the period 1981--2024. To address the challenge of early-season data availability, we harmonized agronomic records with meteorological reanalysis and seasonal forecasts.

\textbf{Agronomic Data.} Yield data ($Y_{d,t}$) was constructed from the Thuringian State Institute archives and the Federal Statistical Office (Destatis). Spatial masking was performed using the DLR CropMap \citep{dlr_cropmap} to ensure climate signals were aggregated strictly from relevant agricultural pixels.

\textbf{Meteorological Forcing.} We utilized AgERA5 \citep{agera5} for observed daily surface meteorology. For operational inference (March 1st), we integrated ECMWF SEAS5 seasonal forecasts \citep{ecmwf_seas5}. To mitigate the coarse resolution of seasonal forecasts, an Analog Ensemble approach was employed: SEAS5 anomalies selected similar historical AgERA5 years to drive the process-based simulations.

\textbf{Environmental Covariates.} Dynamic soil moisture and land surface temperatures were derived from ERA5-Land \citep{era5land}, supplemented by static soil properties from SoilGrids 250m \citep{hengl2017soilgrids250m}.

\subsubsection{Non-Climatic Noise Filtering}
To ensure the system learns from climatic signals rather than data artifacts, we applied a noise-reduction filter. Observations where the minimum error achievable by any component model exceeded 200 dt/ha were flagged as likely data entry errors or non-climatic failures (e.g., local pest outbreaks) and excluded from the training set. An ablation study was performed to verify that this filter did not introduce bias into the final skill scores.

\subsection{The Statistical Trend Baseline}
The baseline forecast $\hat{y}_{\text{base}}(t)$ is composed of a long-term trend and a short-term residual correction, executed via strict Walk-Forward Validation. The trend is modeled using a Generalized Additive Model (GAM) with a smoothing spline, while residuals are modeled using an ARIMA(1, 0, 0) process to capture temporal autocorrelation.

\subsection{Feature Selection and Engineering}
We employ a two-stage pipeline designed to translate raw environmental data into agronomically meaningful signals, focusing on physiological stress limits.

\subsubsection{Bio-Physical Stress Markers}
Beyond standard weather variables, we engineered three markers to detect regime-switching triggers:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Critical Dryness Index (CASDI):} Monitors root-zone water exhaustion by simulating the interaction between cumulative precipitation deficits and soil water holding capacity.
    \item \textbf{Night-time Metabolic Stress Detector (NMSD):} Tracks high minimum temperatures during the summer months, which increase dark respiration and deplete accumulated biomass.
    \item \textbf{Oxygen Stress (OSAW):} Identifies periods of simulated soil saturation exceeding field capacity, inhibiting root respiration (Anoxia).
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Climate Indices and Stress Vectors}
To capture the magnitude of extreme events without data sparsity, we utilize recursive Z-score normalization. For a feature $x$ at year $t$, the Z-score is calculated using only historical stats $\mu_{1:t-1}$ and $\sigma_{1:t-1}$ to prevent data leakage. These are combined into a \textbf{Compound Failure Index} ($I_{\text{fail}}$) aggregating stress vectors for Scorch, Waterlogging, and Late Sowing.

\subsection{Predictive Algorithms}
We implemented the machine learning components using Scikit-Learn \citep{scikitlearn} and XGBoost \citep{chen2016xgboost}.

\textbf{Multivariate Heat Signal.} This model predicts cumulative heat exposure ($T_{max} > 25^{\circ}C$) using March 1st precursors (e.g., Spring soil temperature and solar radiation). To ensure operational integrity, the model employs \textbf{Operational Scaling}: volatility expansion factors are calculated strictly using the variance of the training set to project conservative raw outputs into a biologically realistic range, avoiding look-ahead bias.

\textbf{Physics-Informed Ensemble.} This component targets the 0.20 and 0.80 quantiles, defining the yield distribution's floor and ceiling. It blends the statistical trend with quantile predictions based on the simulated WOFOST 7.2 biomass ratio.

\textbf{Robust Linear Integrator.} This utilizes a HuberRegressor ($\epsilon=1.35$) to predict yield residuals, maintaining independence from the global baseline. Inputs include WOFOST biomass residuals, root-zone depletion, and biological risk proxies.

\subsection{Super Ensemble Architecture}
The final system is a Super Ensemble driven by a Meta-Learner (XGBoost Classifier) functioning as a dynamic gating network.

\subsubsection{Regret-Weighted Training}
To prioritize the mitigation of extreme errors, the Meta-Learner employs a \textbf{Regret-Weighted Training} scheme. Training samples are weighted by the 'Regret' ($w_t$), defined as the difference between the median ensemble error and the oracle (best-case) error for that district-year:
\begin{equation}
    w_t = \text{Error}_{\text{median}} - \text{Error}_{\text{oracle}}
\end{equation}
This forces the classifier to focus on atmospheric patterns that typically precede catastrophic failures of the statistical trend.

\subsubsection{Inference via Soft Voting}
The classifier outputs a probability distribution over the available sub-models. The final yield forecast $\hat{Y}_{d,t}$ is the probability-weighted sum of the component predictions:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{Y}_{d,t} = \sum_{k=1}^{K} P(M_k | \mathbf{x}_{d,t}) \cdot \hat{y}_k(d,t)
\end{equation}
where $P(M_k | \mathbf{x}_{d,t})$ is the probability assigned to model $M_k$. This allows for continuous transition between the Trend and Physics regimes.
