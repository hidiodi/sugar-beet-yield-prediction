\section{Results}
Validation over 2000--2024 ($N=5041$) using SEAS5 hindcasts (Table 1) confirms the Super Ensemble's superiority. While absolute predictive power ($R^2$) naturally declines during the volatile 2010--2024 period due to increased stochasticity, the model's Skill Score actually improves from \textbf{16.25\% to 18.27\%}, demonstrating that physics-informed switching becomes more valuable as climate noise increases.

\subsection{Dynamics and Ablation}
Forensic analysis confirms that $M_3$ (Hybrid) specialists are prioritized during extreme yield crashes, triggered by the Meta-Learner's detection of heat and water stress markers. The probabilistic integration achieved an MAE of 56.29~dt/ha. In contrast, a "Hard Switching" approach (selecting only the single most probable model) resulted in an MAE of 69.19~dt/ha. This confirms that early-season forecasting is more effectively handled as a blending of regimes rather than a discrete binary choice.

Systemic component failure accounts for 71.7\% of the remaining error, while selection error (sub-optimal probability assignment) represents only 28.3\% of the total residual. Ablation results (Fig~\ref{fig:ablation}) confirm that removing bio-physical stress signals from the integration logic increases 2018 drought error significantly, while the exclusion of the outlier filter changed overall skill by $<0.1\%$.
