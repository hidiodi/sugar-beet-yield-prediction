    \begin{abstract}
      In an era of increasing climate volatility, early-season crop yield forecasting faces a critical dilemma: statistical models often fail to extrapolate to unprecedented weather events, while process-based models struggle with calibration and initial condition uncertainty. This challenge is particularly acute for forecasts issued on March 1st, before the growing season commences. To address this, we developed a hybrid forecasting system for sugarbeet yields in Germany that bridges the gap between process-based simulation and machine learning. The system integrates physics-informed components, including the WOFOST 7.2 simulation and a specialized Multivariate Heat Signal model (serving as the $S_{hybrid}$ generator, $r=0.63$), within an Expert System-Guided Ensemble architecture. An Expert System, driven by this bio-physical stress signal, dynamically switches between three specialized components via threshold switching, effectively transitions between ``Normal Regimes'' and ``Extreme Stress Regimes'' based on emerging bio-physical markers such as summer water balance anomalies, heat stress days, and anoxia events. Validated via strict time-dependent cross-validation over the 2000--2024 period ($N=5041$), the Expert System-Guided Ensemble achieved a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 61.79 dt/ha, representing an 8.06\% improvement in skill over a robust Statistical Trend baseline. Notably, in the recent volatile period (2010--2024), the relative skill gain increased to 10.75\%. Crucially, the system demonstrated a 32.0\% reduction in absolute error during the catastrophic 2018 drought, providing actionable risk signals 90 days before satellite-based monitoring indices become viable.
      \vspace{1cm}
    \end{abstract}
  \end{@twocolumnfalse}
