    \begin{abstract}
      In an era of increasing climate volatility, early-season crop yield forecasting faces a critical dilemma: statistical models often fail to extrapolate to unprecedented weather events, while process-based models struggle with calibration and initial condition uncertainty. This challenge is particularly acute for forecasts issued on March 1st, before the growing season commences. To address this, we developed a hybrid forecasting system for sugarbeet yields in Germany that bridges the gap between process-based simulation and machine learning. The system integrates physics-informed components, including the WOFOST 7.2 simulation and a specialized Multivariate Heat Signal model, within a \textbf{Physically-Gated Anomaly Detector} architecture. An \textbf{Asymmetric Threshold Gate}, driven by bio-physical stress signals, dynamically switches between a statistical baseline and a hybrid detector, effectively transitioning between ``Normal Regimes'' and ``Extreme Stress Regimes''. We rigorously purged all information unavailable on March 1st to ensure a realistic ``Leak-Free'' production forecast. Validated via strict time-dependent cross-validation over the 2005--2024 period ($N=3794$), the Physically-Gated Anomaly Detector achieved a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 64.99 dt/ha, representing a consistent +7.33\% improvement in skill ($p < 0.001$, Diebold-Mariano) over a robust Statistical Trend baseline. Notably, in the recent volatile period (2014--2024), the relative skill gain increased to +8.57\% (MAE 83.3 vs 91.1), successfully identifying the 2018 drought signal while avoiding false alarms in bumper years like 2014.
      \vspace{1cm}
    \end{abstract}
  \end{@twocolumnfalse}
